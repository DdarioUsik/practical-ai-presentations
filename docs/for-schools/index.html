Professional development for private schools

Give your school team four weeks to put AI into practice.

Teachers, academic leads and administrators each build and test an AI assistant for a task they already do.

What your team takes away AI assistants built for school work

Each participant leaves with a tested workflow and a way to check its output.

Start with the work each team already does

Teachers

Draft differentiated assignments, assessment criteria and feedback. Teachers check each draft against the curriculum before using it.

Academic leads

Organise teaching materials, prepare reports and review anonymised information about learning. Academic leads check the conclusions against school records.

Leadership and administration

Summarise approved documents, prepare questions for meetings and draft internal guidance. School leaders review the evidence and make the decisions.

Two examples from a school workday

Both examples use fictional data.

For a teacher

Water cycle activities for Year 5

The task

Design three activities for the same learning objective, at different levels of difficulty.

AI draft
  1. Foundation: put the stages of the water cycle in order.
  2. Core: explain how water from a puddle evaporates and becomes part of a cloud.
  3. Extension: design an experiment that demonstrates evaporation and condensation.
Teacher review

Check each activity against the lesson objective, pupils’ age and the curriculum. Adjust the wording where needed.

For a school director

Prepare questions for a school budget review

The task

Use an approved spreadsheet with planned and actual spending to prepare a short note for the budget meeting.

Excerpt from the AI draft

“Spending on teaching materials was 6 units over budget. Ask the person responsible when the purchases were made and what drove the difference.”

Director review

Check the figure against the spreadsheet, confirm the context and decide which questions to raise.

Four weeks from task to tested assistant

Before training, we interview school leaders and staff, identify priorities and agree the tools and data participants can use.

Week 1

Choose the task

Agree what a good result looks like.

Week 2

Build the assistant

Prepare instructions and approved examples.

Week 3

Try it in practice

Check outputs against the original materials and refine the workflow.

Week 4

Show how it works

Demonstrate the assistant and explain how colleagues can review its output.
